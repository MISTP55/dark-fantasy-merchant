# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## The game

Dark Fantasy Merchant is a maritime trade management and simulation game. The player is a merchant in a dark fantasy world made of coastal cities that trade with each other by sea and by river. The goals are to build a maritime trading empire and to rise through the social hierarchy.

- **Presentation**: 2D pixel art.
- **Platform**: PC only (keyboard and mouse).

## Project state

Unity **6000.6.4f1** project, created from the URP 3D template and then converted in place to 2D. The world map is the first subsystem, the player has one ship that sails on it over the navigable areas painted on the map and can lie in the port of a city, the ship has a crew of sailors that sets its speed, the world has a date that passes, with a fast forward, the player has a treasury of gold, pays the wages of the sailors out of it every week, notifications tell the player what happens (so far, a ship that arrives and the expenses of a week), and cities hold stocks of goods (the twenty of Patrician III, counted in barrels or in bushels) that they consume and produce in workshops regulated by their stocks, which a ship in port buys into its hold and sells elsewhere, at prices that follow the stocks, the population of a city follows its jobs and houses, an AI merchant ship per city trades between the cities on their own, and a debug tool lists the cities with the figures of their economy and the ships with what they are doing; nothing else of the game exists yet. `TutorialInfo/` and `SampleScene` are template leftovers and not part of the game. Update this file as the architecture grows.

## Architecture

Code is split into assemblies, each with its own `.asmdef`:

| Assembly | Folder | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `DarkFantasyMerchant.Core` | `Assets/Scripts/Core` | Plain C# logic and runtime state. No `MonoBehaviour`, nothing that needs a scene. |
| `DarkFantasyMerchant.Game` | `Assets/Scripts/Game` | ScriptableObject definitions and thin MonoBehaviour adapters over `Core`. |
| `DarkFantasyMerchant.Editor` | `Assets/Scripts/Editor` | Editor-only tools. |
| `DarkFantasyMerchant.Tests.EditMode` | `Assets/Tests/EditMode` | EditMode tests for `Core`. |

Content lives in `Assets/Data`, runtime UI in `Assets/UI`, art in `Assets/Art`, prefabs in `Assets/Prefabs`.

### World map

- Positions on the map are **normalized** (`(0,0)` bottom-left, `(1,1)` top-right) and converted to world space only through `MapProjection`. The map is centered on the world origin and sized by `WorldMapDefinition.worldWidth`, so the map image can be replaced by one of another resolution without moving anything.
- `Assets/Art/WorldMap/WorldMap.jpg` is a placeholder for future pixel art.
- `WorldMapView` owns the scene's `MapSelectionState<CityDefinition>` (hovered and selected city). Markers and the UI panel both react to it and do not know about each other.
- The panel of the selected city is centered on the screen (by its root, in `CityInfoPanel.uss`), over the map and possibly over the city itself: the top right corner belongs to the treasury.
- `WorldMapInput` is the only reader of the `WorldMap` action map; it raises click, drag and zoom events and ignores pointer input that starts over the UI.
- `MapCameraModel` is where input has sent the camera (the target); `MapCameraSmoother` is what is displayed, eased towards the target. Position and size share one easing factor on purpose: that keeps the zoom anchor fixed during the transition, so do not split it into separate pan and zoom settings. Hover, picking and marker size follow the displayed view.
- Camera clamping and zoom math are in `MapCameraModel`; picking is `CityPicker` (nearest city within a screen-pixel radius, no colliders).
- A city stands on a navigable cell of the mask, at the edge of the water beside the town drawn for it on the map. The order of the map's cities is the order of the debug tables and of the AI merchants' numbers, and the player starts in the first, Bruges.
- Cities are placed by dragging their handles in the Scene view (`CityPlacementTool`). It and the navigation mask tool find the map to work on through `WorldMapEditorContext`.
- `Tools > Dark Fantasy Merchant > Build World Map Scene` (`WorldMapSetup.Build`) recreates the scene, the marker, ship and ship route prefabs, the panel settings, the map definition, the ship definition, the calendar, the player start, the expenses, the goods, the economy or the AI merchants when one is missing, and leaves existing ones untouched. The exceptions are the `Ships` object with the `ShipRoute` object under it, the `Ship Panel`, `World Clock`, `Time HUD`, `Player Treasury`, `Treasury HUD`, `Player Notifications`, `Notifications HUD`, `Ship Arrival Notifier`, `Player Expenses`, `World Economy`, `AI Merchants` and `Debug Panel` objects: they are also added to an existing scene that has none, as are the empty references to them (`shipsView` of `WorldMapInteraction`, `CityInfoPanelController` and `ShipInfoPanelController`, `routeView` and `playerStart` of `ShipsView`, `worldClock` and `economy` of `ShipsView`, `WorldMapInteraction` and `WorldMapCameraController`, `playerTreasury` of `TreasuryHudController`, `playerNotifications` of `NotificationsHudController`, `shipsView` and `playerNotifications` of `ShipArrivalNotifier`, `expenses`, `worldClock`, `playerTreasury`, `shipsView` and `playerNotifications` of `PlayerExpenses`, `economy`, `mapView` and `worldClock` of `WorldEconomy`, `worldEconomy` and `playerTreasury` of `CityInfoPanelController`, `economy` of `ShipInfoPanelController`, `definition`, `shipsView`, `worldEconomy`, `mapView` and `worldClock` of `AiMerchants`, `worldEconomy`, `mapView`, `input`, `shipsView` and `aiMerchants` of `DebugPanelController`), and that scene is saved unless it already had unsaved changes. Cities of the map that produce nothing are given the productions of the sample content, whether the tool has just created them or not. The tool offers to save the open scene first, and adds `WorldMap.unity` to the build list without removing other scenes. Sample cities are only recreated together with a missing map definition.

### Ships

- `Ship` (Core) is the runtime state of one ship: normalized position, destination, heading. `ShipDefinition` (Game) is its static definition: name, speed in world units per second, crew capacity (see Crew), and eight sprites indexed by `CompassDirection` (`N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW`, the order of the sprite sheets).
- A ship follows a route: a list of waypoints, exposed as `RemainingWaypoints`, with `Destination` the last one. `SetDestination` asks the ship's `NavigationPathfinder` for the route, so the ship sails over water only; without a pathfinder (a map with no mask) the route is one straight leg. What is left of a step after a waypoint is spent on the next leg, so the speed is constant through turns.
- Steps are measured in world space through `MapProjection`, not in normalized space, so the speed is the same in every direction on a map that is not square.
- A ship is created on the water nearest to the position it is given: cities are on land, so the player ship starts beside its start city, not on it.
- `ShipsView` owns the ships, their `ShipView`s and a `MapSelectionState<Ship>`, and advances the ships with the world clock's `DeltaTime` (see Time), or with `Time.deltaTime` in a scene without a clock. It holds a list although there is a single player ship, which starts on `startCity` or the first city of the map.
- `ShipsView` holds every ship of the map, the player's and the others, and knows which are the player's (`PlayerShips`, `IsPlayerShip`). `RemoveShip` takes any ship off the map for good, from every one of its lists at once (they are parallel, by index), and `Contains` says whether a ship is still on it. `SpawnAiShip` adds a ship the player does not own, with its own name and a tint that replaces the normal color of its `ShipView`; a ship's name is its own (`DisplayNameOf`), not its definition's, since several ships share one. Every ship is hovered, selected, shown in the ship panel and has its route drawn alike. Only the player's ships take the player's orders, are awaited by a fast forward, are paid wages and have their arrivals announced.
- `WorldMapInteraction` arbitrates between the two selection states: a ship is picked before the cities, and selecting one clears the other, so at most one thing is hovered and one selected (the one exception is a ship in port, see below). Pressing the right button sends the selected ship to the pointer, at once and without waiting for the release (an order given while the pointer moves must not be lost), or to the nearest water it can reach when that point is on land or in another sea. When the pointer is on a city, the ship is sent to that city's port instead.
- `ShipInfoPanelController` shows the panel of the selected ship (`Assets/UI/WorldMap/ShipInfoPanel.uxml`: its name, its crew, its hold and a close button that deselects it), in a fourth `UIDocument` on the same `PanelSettings`, so the map does not take a click on it. It is at the bottom left of the screen, not centered like the city panel: the selected ship is ordered about on the map. It shows a ship selected in port too, beside the panel of its city. The two panels share their frame, title and close button styles (`InfoPanel.uss`).
- Ships keep a constant on-screen size, like city markers, and are picked with `CityPicker` on their current world positions.
- The route of the selected ship is drawn on the map while it is under way (`ShipRouteView`, driven by `ShipsView`): a solid line over what it has sailed, a dashed one over what is left, and a marker where the route ends, unless the route enters a city's port. `Ship.SailedWaypoints` is what the solid line follows: where the ship was when it was ordered, then the waypoints it reached. Every order the ship takes starts it again and it is empty when idle, so an idle ship, or one in port, has no route to show.
- The two lines are `LineRenderer`s with an unlit sprite material, drawn above the map and below the city markers, with widths and dashes in screen pixels. The dashes are a repeated two-pixel texture laid out from the destination, not from the ship (the dashed line's points run from the destination back to the ship), so they do not slide while it sails (they do rescale around the destination when zooming).

### Crew

- `ShipCrew` (Core) is the sailors aboard one ship (`Count`) out of those it has room for (`Capacity`); `Ship.Crew` holds it. Nothing changes a crew yet: sailors will be hired in cities. Every sailor aboard is paid a weekly wage (see Expenses).
- The crew sets the share of its speed a ship sails at (`SpeedFactor`), from the occupancy (`Count / Capacity`): half speed up to 10 % of the capacity, then rising in a straight line to full speed at 50 %. More sailors do not make it faster. A ship without a sailor does not move and keeps its route: it stays under way, so it never enters the port it was ordered to, and a fast forward that awaits it ends only on input. `Ship.Advance` reads the factor on every step, and `ShipDefinition.Speed` is the speed at a factor of 1.
- `ShipDefinition.crewCapacity` is the capacity of a kind of ship (28 for the merchant ship); `PlayerStartDefinition.startingCrew` (12) is what the player ship starts with, cut down to the capacity with a warning when it exceeds it (`ShipsView` also warns of a start without a sailor). `ShipsView` reads it through its `playerStart` reference, which is optional: without it the ship starts with a full crew.
- The panel of the selected ship shows the crew ("Équipage : 12 / 28", see Ships). It is written on selection: a crew that changes will need an event to refresh it.

### Ports

- `ShipDocking<TPort>` (Core, generic like `MapSelectionState<T>`) says which ships lie in which port and which are sailing to one. `ShipsView` owns the one of the map, with `CityDefinition` as the port, and updates it after the ships have advanced. Orders go through it (`OrderToPort`, `OrderToPoint`), not straight to `Ship.SetDestination`.
- A ship enters a port only when it was ordered to that city and has arrived: an order to the water beside a city does not dock, and any other order under way cancels the entry. A ship in port leaves on its next order, except one to its own city. The ship starts at sea, beside its start city.
- A port is entered from `Ship.AnchorageAt(city position)`, where a ship that starts on that city is put. A city the ship cannot sail to is not entered: the ship stops as near as it can and stays at sea.
- A ship in port stays where it anchored but is not on the map: its view is inactive, its entry in `ShipWorldPositions` is NaN (which `CityPicker` skips), and it is deselected when it arrives.
- The city panel lists the ships in port (`CityInfoPanelController`, which reads `ShipsView`). Clicking a row selects the ship while its city stays selected, the only case where two things are selected; `WorldMapInteraction` clears that ship selection as soon as another city, or none, is selected. A right click then orders the ship out, which closes the panel. The ships of the AI merchants are listed and selected there too, but the market stays read-only for them, and the panel also closes when such a ship leaves the port by itself while it is selected (`ShipDocking.Undocked`).

### Navigation mask

- `NavigationGrid` (Core) says which cells of the map ships can sail on: one bit per cell, cell `(0,0)` bottom-left like normalized positions, queried by cell or by normalized point. It also holds the painting logic (disc, stroke, 4-connected fill, resample). `NavigationMaskDefinition` (Game) stores a grid's size and bits and is referenced by `WorldMapDefinition.navigationMask`; a map without a mask is valid.
- `ShipsView` builds one `NavigationPathfinder` from the mask at startup and gives it to its ships. The mask is not expected to change while the game runs.
- The grid is defined over normalized space, 1024 cells wide by default, with a height that follows the map's aspect ratio so cells are square. Replacing the map image by one of another shape needs a **Resize** in the tool, not a repaint.
- The mask is painted in the Scene view (2D mode) with the **Navigation Mask** tool of the Scene view toolbar (`NavigationMaskTool`, settings in the `NavigationMaskOverlay` panel): brush, eraser, fill bucket, and a detection that marks low-saturation pixels of the map image as water (`WaterColorClassifier`). Shift swaps brush and eraser; `[` and `]` resize the brush. While this tool is active, `CityPlacementTool` draws no handles.
- `NavigationMaskSession` is the tool's working copy of the grid. It is written to the asset once per stroke through `NavigationMaskAuthoring.Apply`, which is what makes a stroke one undo step, and rebuilt from the asset after an undo or redo, or at the start of an edit when the asset was changed by something else (`SyncWithAsset`). Its preview texture is rewritten only where a stroke passed; do not go back to rebuilding it whole on every drag event.
- The mask's fields are hidden in the Inspector on purpose: its size and bits only make sense together.

### Pathfinding

- Movement rules, shared by the search and by route smoothing: a ship moves to a side neighbour, or to a diagonal one only when both cells beside the move are navigable; a straight leg is allowed only when every cell it touches, even by a corner, is navigable (`NavigationLineOfSight`, with a margin of a thousandth of a cell because positions are floats). A diagonal coastline one cell thick therefore holds, as it does for the fill bucket, and two cells are reachable from one another exactly when they are in the same 4-connected region.
- `NavigationCellSearch` is an A* over the cells; its buffers are allocated once and reused, so an order allocates nothing. `NavigationPathfinder` labels the regions, picks the start and goal cells, runs the search and drops the waypoints a straight leg can skip.
- An order ends on the clicked point itself when it is on water the ship can reach, otherwise on the center of the nearest cell of the ship's own region.
- A search runs synchronously on the click. About 16 bytes per cell are held for the regions and the search, about 14 MB for a 1024-cell-wide grid.

### Time

- `GameClock` (Core) is the time of the simulated world: `Advance(real seconds)` gives `DeltaTime`, the simulated seconds of the frame, multiplied in fast forward, and raises `DayStarted` once per day that starts, even when one step crosses several. Everything simulated is advanced with that `DeltaTime`. `Time.timeScale` stays at 1, so the camera and the UI stay in real time; do not speed the game up through it.
- `GameDate` (Core) is a day of the calendar, derived from the days elapsed. `CalendarDefinition` (Game, `Assets/Data/Calendar/Calendar.asset`) holds the start year (932), the month names (12 invented ones; their number is the number of months), the days per month (30), the days per week (7, see Expenses), the real seconds per day (30) and the fast forward multiplier (60), and formats a date.
- `WorldClock` owns the clock and advances it first in the frame (execution order -100). It is optional for `ShipsView`, `WorldMapInteraction` and `WorldMapCameraController`, which work as before without it; `PlayerExpenses`, `WorldEconomy` and `AiMerchants` need it.
- Fast forward is a mode in which the map is only watched. `WorldMapInteraction` clears hover and selection when it starts, allows none while it lasts, and ends it on `WorldMapInput.AnyInput`: any keyboard key but the one of the debug tools (see Debug tools), or a mouse button or wheel step on the map. It then calls `ConsumeInput`, so that the same input gives no click, order or zoom, not even through the release of the button; a button that stays held can still drag the map (`PointerGesture.PressWithoutClick`). The HUD's button is over the UI: it does not raise `AnyInput` and toggles the mode itself. Fast forward also ends by itself when the ship that was selected and under way when it started arrives (`awaitedShip`). However it ends, the ship that was selected when it started is selected again, or the city whose port it has entered meanwhile.
- `WorldMapCameraController` saves the view when fast forward starts, shows the whole map, ignores the input while it lasts and returns to the saved view afterwards. When it is the arrival of the awaited ship that ends the fast forward, `WorldMapInteraction` then replaces that view by a close-up of where the ship arrived (`ShowCloseUp`: `MapCameraModel.ZoomInFullyOn`, the smallest size, centered on the ship as far as the map allows). These changes of view are instant (`MapCameraSmoother.SnapTo`), unlike every other move of the camera.
- `TimeHudController` shows the date and the button (`Assets/UI/WorldMap/TimeHud.uxml`), in a second `UIDocument` that uses the same `PanelSettings` as the city panel: one panel, so `CityInfoPanelController.IsPointerOverUi` sees the button too. The date label is rewritten when a day starts, not every frame.
- Keyboard keys are read from `Keyboard.current.allKeys`, not from an action: the "any key" control does not see a key pressed while another is held.

### Treasury

- `Treasury` (Core) is the player's gold, in whole coins. It can be negative: the player is then in debt (`IsInDebt`). `Withdraw` always takes the amount and is for what is paid whatever the gold left (wages); `TryWithdraw` takes it only when `CanAfford` and is for what a lack of gold must block (buying goods). `Changed` is raised with the new gold, only on an actual change. Selling goods deposits and buying them withdraws (see Economy); the other withdrawal is the weekly expenses (see Expenses).
- `PlayerStartDefinition` (Game, `Assets/Data/Player/PlayerStart.asset`) holds the starting gold and the starting crew of the player ship (see Crew). `PlayerTreasury` owns the treasury and creates it in `Awake`, before the components that read it (execution order -100, like `WorldClock`).
- `TreasuryHudController` shows the gold at the top right of the screen (`Assets/UI/WorldMap/TreasuryHud.uxml`, "10 000 or", in red when in debt), in a third `UIDocument` on the same `PanelSettings`. Its label ignores the pointer, so the map under it stays reachable, and is rewritten on `Changed`, not every frame.

### Notifications

- `NotificationFeed` (Core) is the messages shown to the player, the newest first. `Post(text)` adds one; `Advance(seconds)` ages them: a `Notification` is opaque for the display time, its `Opacity` then falls in a straight line to 0 over the fade, and it is removed (`Removed`, the oldest first). There is no limit to their number and the same text posted twice gives two.
- `PlayerNotifications` owns the feed and creates it in `Awake` (execution order -100, like `PlayerTreasury`), with the display time (5 s) and the fade (1 s) as its own serialized fields. It ages the feed in real time, not with the world clock: a notification is read by the player, in fast forward too.
- `NotificationsHudController` shows them under the treasury (`Assets/UI/WorldMap/NotificationsHud.uxml`), in a fifth `UIDocument` on the same `PanelSettings`: one label per notification, inserted at the top, so the older ones move down. The labels ignore the pointer, and only the opacity of a fading one is rewritten. The list's `top` in `NotificationsHud.uss` follows the height of the treasury label: change them together.
- Anything can post to the feed. The sources so far are `PlayerExpenses` (see Expenses) and `ShipArrivalNotifier`, which listens to `ShipsView.ShipArrived`: "Navire marchand a jeté l'ancre à Bruges." for a ship that entered a port, "Navire marchand a atteint sa destination." for one that stopped at sea (also a ship sent to a city it cannot reach). These texts have no article and nothing that agrees with the ship's name or elides before the city's; keep new ones that way.
- `Ship.Arrived` is raised when a ship reaches the end of its route, not when an order replaces the route. `ShipArrivals<TPort>` (Core) turns it into arrivals with their port: it also counts a ship that enters a port without sailing (ordered to the city it was already beside, which raises no `Ship.Arrived`), each ship once, and holds them until `Report`. `ShipsView` calls `Report` after `Docking.Update`, so the port is known, and raises `ShipArrived` with the city, or null.

### Expenses

- `WeeklyBilling` (Core) makes the player pay the recurring expenses every week. `StartDay(elapsed days)` is called for every day that starts; when the day after a week's last one starts, it takes the `WeeklyBill` from the treasury with `Withdraw`, so a player without the gold goes into debt, and raises `Paid` with the bill. A week in which nothing is owed takes nothing and raises nothing.
- Weeks are counted from the first day of the game (`CalendarDefinition.daysPerWeek`, 7), not within a month, which is not a whole number of weeks: the first payment is made when the 8th day starts. The billing keeps no count of its own: the week is read from the clock's elapsed days, so a day it is not told of shifts no week. A fast forward step that crosses several weeks pays each of them.
- `WeeklyBill` (Core) is what is owed for one week, one amount per kind of expense, and their `Total`. The only one so far is `SailorWages`: the weekly wage for every sailor aboard the player's ships when the week ends, at sea or in port. A new recurring expense is a new amount in the bill, added to the total.
- `ExpensesDefinition` (Game, `Assets/Data/Economy/Expenses.asset`) holds the weekly wage of a sailor (10).
- `PlayerExpenses` owns the billing, drives it with `GameClock.DayStarted` and counts the sailors of `ShipsView.PlayerShips`. Unlike the clock, it is not optional for the game, but its `playerNotifications` reference is: with it, a payment posts "Dépenses de la semaine : 120 or.", the gold written by `TreasuryHudController.FormatGold`.

### Economy

- Goods are Patrician III's twenty, in its order (grain, meat, fish, beer, salt, honey, spices, wine, cloth, skins, oil, timber, metal, leather, wool, pitch, pig iron, hemp, pottery, tiles), with its base prices, which can be fractions (34.8 for beer). A good is counted in its unit (`GoodUnit`): barrels ("tonneaux"), or bushels ("boisseaux", Patrician III's loads) for grain, meat, fish, timber, wool, pig iron, hemp and tiles. Stocks, consumption, production, thresholds and prices are in the good's unit: the price of grain is that of a bushel. Consumption per 1 000 inhabitants is Patrician III's per class, averaged over its starting mix (10 % rich, 30 % wealthy, 60 % poor). Pitch and pig iron are consumed by no one until workshops have inputs. `GoodDefinition` (Game, `Assets/Data/Goods`) is a good: its name, its category (`GoodCategory`: `Food` for grain, meat, fish and beer, `RawMaterial` for the others, which is what a new good is; it no longer changes anything), its unit, its base price, what 1 000 inhabitants consume of it in a week (spread over the days of the calendar's week), the maximum of workers and the daily output of a worker of the workshop that produces it, and the weight the AI merchants give it (see AI merchants). `EconomyDefinition` (`Assets/Data/Economy/Economy.asset`) holds the catalogue, whose order is the order of every list of goods on screen, and the settings below. In `Core` a good is its index among the active goods.
- Every good of the catalogue is a `CatalogueGood`: the good and whether it is active, a box ticked or not before it on its line of the Inspector (`CatalogueGoodDrawer`), set before Play mode. A good that is not active does not exist in the game: `EconomyDefinition.Goods`, which everything reads, holds only the active goods, in the order of the catalogue, so they are what the markets, the holds, the AI merchants and every list on screen have; `Catalogue` is every good, for the Editor and the content tests, and `IsInCatalogue` tells a good switched off from one that is not listed. A city keeps its workshop for an inactive good in its `CityDefinition` but has none in the game: the starting workforce is spread over its other workshops, and a city left with none is warned about by `WorldEconomy` and still starts with the economy's population. A catalogue with no active good is refused like an empty one (`TryValidate`, also warned about in the Inspector). The goods stay in the catalogue and come back as they were when they are switched on again.
- `CityDefinition` holds the goods the city has a workshop for, efficiently and inefficiently; it depends on trade for the others. The map's 24 cities are Patrician III's standard towns with the goods they produce there (`patrician3-re\resultats\Villes de la carte.md`): six to ten workshops, three to six of them efficient. Patrician III's cattle makes both meat and leather, its whale oil comes with an inefficient fishery, and its hidden supplies of skins, wine and salt are inefficient workshops. Every active good is produced efficiently somewhere; spices, inactive, are produced nowhere, as in Patrician III. Its Inspector (`CityDefinitionEditor`) does not show the two lists but every good of the economy's catalogue by its asset's name, inactive ones greyed, with three radio buttons in columns headed "Not produced", "Efficient" and "Inefficient" (the Editor is in English), which write the lists (`CityProductionEditing.SetProduction`, which also drops their empty entries); a good of the lists outside the catalogue is listed after, to be taken out. Without an economy asset, the lists are shown as they are. `WorldEconomy` owns one `CityMarket` (Core) per city of the map, created in `Start` (the map view lists its cities in `Awake`), and calls their `StartDay` on `GameClock.DayStarted`.
- Workshops follow Patrician III (`patrician3-re\resultats\Production et consommation des villes.md`). A city that produces a good has a workshop for it: workers up to the good's maximum (`GoodDefinition.maxWorkers`, the same in every city), each making the good's daily output (`dailyOutputPerWorker`), times 0.75 in a city that produces it inefficiently (`EconomyDefinition.inefficientEfficiency`). Its capacity does not depend on the size of the city. A day of a city, per good (`CityMarket.StartDay`): the inhabitants consume, down to nothing; then the workshop hires by where the stock left stands against the thresholds (`WorkforceRule`): 16 workers at T0 or under, 8 above T0, 4 above T1, 1 above T2, never above its maximum, and produces with the workers of the day; above T3 it keeps a single worker and produces nothing that day. A stock equal to a threshold counts as under it. A producer left alone therefore settles between T2 and T3. There is no limit to what a city stores: what the player delivers always adds, and a stock above T3 is only consumed. Stocks are fractional; what is shown and can be bought is the whole part.
- A new game starts as in Patrician III: every city gets the same workforce (`EconomyDefinition.startingWorkforce`, 250), spread evenly over its workshops, rounded up and cut to their maximum (`WorkforceRule.StartingWorkersOf`), and the same population, the workforce times the inhabitants per worker (`inhabitantsPerWorker`, 4: Patrician III's), 1 000. Workers are a count per workshop. A market given a `LabourRule` takes them from its beggars and its reserves (see Population), so they are no longer free; `WorldEconomy` does not give one yet, so in the game hiring is still immediate, workers are not taken from the inhabitants and laid-off workers vanish. A city holds at the start the same days of its consumption of every good, produced or not (`startingStockDays`, 7): T0 where the floor of 5 units does not apply, so prices open at 1.5 and 1.4 times the base price; a good the city consumes little starts under its floor, so higher, or with no whole unit to sell. Producers fill up and the others run out within the first week.
- Prices follow Patrician III (`patrician3-re\resultats\Calcul des prix.md`): a good's base price times a factor of the city's stock. `ThresholdRule` (Core) gives the four thresholds of a good in a city (`CityMarket.ThresholdsOf`, a `StockThresholds`), worked out when a price is asked so that they follow the population: T0 = 7 days of consumption, T1 = T0 + 14 days of consumption, T2 = T1 + 10 days of the capacity of the city's workshop (T1 itself for a good it does not produce), T3 = T2 + T0, each at least its floor (5, 10, 15, 20 units of the good), so a good the city does not consume is priced against the floors. A `PriceCurve` (Core) is five factors, at an empty stock and at T0 to T3, in straight lines between them and constant above T3; two equal thresholds make a step. `MarketPricing` holds two: the player buys at 4.0, 1.5, 1.0, 0.8, 0.6 and sells at 2.0, 1.4, 1.0, 0.7, 0.5, never above the first at any point (checked when it is created). A unit (a barrel or a bushel) is bought at the base price times the average buy factor over the stock it takes away, rounded up, and sold at the average sell factor over the stock it adds, rounded down, so a trade of several units costs the integral of the curve and buying and selling back never gains gold. Every figure is a setting of `EconomyDefinition` (`PriceFactors` for the curves); a setting that breaks these rules (a factor at 0, a rising curve, a sell factor above the buy one, floors out of order, a negative workforce setting, a negative AI weight, a starting population that is not a number) leaves every city without a market, and `EconomyDefinition.TryValidateSettings` names it, in a warning as soon as it is typed in the Inspector (`OnValidate`) and in the error `WorldEconomy` logs. The base prices are balanced by hand in the good assets and no test pins their values.
- `Trade` (Core) buys and sells between a market, a `CargoHold` and the `Treasury`: units are priced one by one, and a trade of several stops at the first that cannot be traded (stock, room in the hold, gold; a treasury in debt buys nothing but still sells). It moves them at once, so the market and the hold raise `Changed` once per trade. Its overloads without a treasury are for the AI merchants, whose gold is not counted: they return what the trade came to.
- `CargoHold` (Core) is the goods aboard a ship, counted in their units, out of its room in barrels (`ShipDefinition.cargoCapacity`, 200 for the merchant ship); a bushel takes `EconomyDefinition.barrelsPerBushel` (10) barrels of room (`GoodUnits`, which `ShipsView` takes from its `economy`). `RoomFor` is the whole units of a good that still fit: a trade never splits a unit, and neither does the AI merchants' planner when it loads a ship (see AI merchants). `Ship.Cargo` holds it.
- Under its description, the city panel names the goods the city produces efficiently ("Production efficace") and inefficiently ("Production inefficace"), separated by commas in the order of the active goods, or "Aucune" (`CityInfoPanelController.ProducedGoodsText`): an inactive good is not named. Without an economy, it names those of the city's lists.
- The city panel shows the population and the market (`CityMarketSection`, fed by `CityInfoPanelController`): stock, buy and sell price of the next unit for every good, each named with its unit ("Céréales (boisseaux)"), in rows that scroll, read-only. When a ship of the player in port is selected in the panel's list, the section also shows that ship's hold, and the prices are the buttons that trade: a click on a buy price buys that good, one on a sell price sells it, 1 unit, Shift 10, Ctrl 100, cut down to what is possible. A price is always a `Button`, disabled whenever its trade is not possible (no ship that trades, nothing aboard to sell, an empty stock, no room for one unit, a lack of gold), and a disabled price is styled as plain text, as readable as the others (`.market__price` in `CityInfoPanel.uss`). The hold column is shown by the `market--trading` class. The section listens to the market, the hold and the treasury while it is shown.
- The ship panel shows the hold ("Cale : 60 / 200 tonneaux") and a line per good aboard with its quantity in its unit ("Céréales : 3 boisseaux"), rewritten on the hold's `Changed`.
- What a city consumes of a good is the good's consumption per 1 000 inhabitants times its population of the moment (`CityMarket.DailyConsumptionOf`), so consumption and the thresholds that are given in days of it follow the population; workshops and stocks do not.
- The output per worker of each good is set so that, every workshop at its maximum, the world of 24 cities can produce 1.5 times what it consumes at the start (1.5 × the world's daily consumption ÷ its producers, an inefficient one counting 0.75, ÷ the good's maximum of workers; pitch and pig iron, consumed by no one, make one unit a day per full efficient workshop): capacity absorbs about eight months of the fastest growth, and the regulation keeps the surplus from piling up. A city that does not produce a good depends on the player or on the AI merchants (see AI merchants) to bring it, and pays high prices for it until then. Every number is a field of an asset, meant to be tuned by playing.

### Population

- `CityPopulation` (Core) is the inhabitants of one city, in whole numbers: the housed ones (`Housed`), the beggars (`Beggars`) and the places of its houses (`Room`, `FreeRoom` never negative), `Inhabitants` being housed plus beggars. The market of the city holds it (`CityMarket.Population`); every city starts with the economy's starting population (see Economy), 4 inhabitants per worker.
- Only the labour of the city changes the population; food no longer makes it grow or decline. `GrowthSinceStart` compares the inhabitants with those of the start.
- The labour is Patrician III's (`patrician3-re\resultats\Population des villes.md`), given to a market by a `LabourRule` (Core: the formulas, stateless). In `CityMarket.StartDay`, after the consumption, each city workshop in the order of the catalogue takes the target of `WorkforceRule.NextWorkers`. Above its workers, it hires the difference, in this order: from its own reserve; from the beggars, 4 beggars becoming 4 housed inhabitants per worker (`inhabitantsPerWorker`), at most a quarter of the beggars and a quarter of the free room (no room, no hiring); from the reserves of the other workshops, in the order of the catalogue. What is still missing is not hired that day. Under its workers, it lays the difference off into its reserve (`ReserveOf`), with no ceiling: they stay housed and still consume. Every layoff adds 2 % of the reserve to a fractional counter of the workshop, whose whole part leaves the reserve for the beggars (4 housed inhabitants each), only while workers and reserve stay under 30. It then produces with its workers.
- After every good, the player's workshops (`AddPlayerWorkshop`, several per good the city produces, each with its jobs, in the order they were built; `PlayerWorkshopsOf`, `PlayerJobsOf`, `PlayerWorkersOf`) fill their vacancies from the beggars: with at least 5 beggars and more than 3 free room, ⌊min(beggars − 1, free room) ÷ 4⌋ workers that day, to the first built up to its jobs, then the next. They never lay off, have no reserve and produce nothing yet. The city therefore hires before the player. The player's houses (`AddPlayerHouse`, `PlayerHouses`) add their room to the city's, for anyone. Each `Add…` raises `Changed` once.
- Last, the beggars come or go: their target (`BeggarTarget`) is √(P × 50 ÷ 2) ÷ 3 + 8, P the inhabitants, every root truncated; under it ⌈√P ÷ 5⌉ come, above it ⌈√P ÷ 10⌉ go, never past it. Beggars are the only source of new inhabitants.
- A market without a `LabourRule` keeps its population: hiring is immediate, laid-off workers vanish, its beggars stay as they are, `BeggarTarget` is 0, and the player's buildings are recorded (a house still adds its room) but their workshops hire nobody.
- The city panel shows the population of the moment, rewritten on the market's `Changed`.

### AI merchants

- A ship per city trades between the cities on their own. Their gold is unlimited: they have no treasury and pay no wages, and only their cumulated profit is counted, for the debug tool. `AiMerchantsDefinition` (Game, `Assets/Data/AI/AiMerchants.asset`) holds the number of ships per city (`shipsPerCity`, 1: 24 ships on the present map), their ship definition (the merchant ship), their tint (a light blue), their crew (14, full speed), the stopover (1 day) and the format of their names ("Marchand indépendant 1" and on, numbered in the order of the cities).
- `AiMerchants` only wires the `Core` classes to the scene. Once the ships view and the economy have started, it measures the travel time between every pair of cities that have a market, a few pairs per frame (4 ms), then spawns `shipsPerCity` ships in the port of every city that reaches at least one other by water, in the order of the cities, and gives them to the fleet: that city becomes the home of its ships, and there is no start draw. A city that reaches no other is warned about and ignored: it is most likely a mistake of the mask. So the AI ships appear a moment after the game starts.
- `PortTravelTable` (Core) is the days between ports, from anchorage to anchorage, infinity when a ship cannot sail from one to the other; `SeaRoutes.TryMeasure` measures one route and says whether it gets there. All AI ships share a definition and a crew, so one table serves them all.
- `AiMerchantFleet<TPort>` (Core, generic like `ShipDocking<TPort>`, to which it listens) is the life of an AI ship, on Patrician III's tour. Every ship has a home city (`HomeOf`), the one it is added in. At home it chooses a final destination (`FinalDestinationOf`) and sails straight to it, with the cargo the planner loads for it, making no stopover on the way out; there it unloads everything, whatever the prices have become, and its final destination becomes home. On the way back it stops at the cities the planner finds worth a short detour, each at most once per return trip, and sells there only what brings the city to its T1, keeping what home lacks (home's T1 less its stock, rounded up); in every port away from home it looks for the next stopover between itself and home. Back home it unloads everything and chooses the next final destination; with none, it waits another stopover and tries again. In every port it stays for the stopover, counted in the world's time; then it loads for its final destination and, with more than a quarter of its hold still free, for its next stopover too; with a quarter or less it drops that stopover and sails to the final destination. A ship found idle at sea is ordered back to the port it last lay in.
- `SupplyPlanner` (Core) decides as Patrician III's merchants do, one good at a time from a source to a destination. It projects the destination's stock on arrival: what it holds, what is under way to it and what it will produce less what it will consume over the days of the trip. `ShipmentQuantity` gives what to carry from the thresholds: nothing when the source is at or under the middle of its T0 and T1 or the destination will be at its T1; the source's surplus above its T1 first, then up to a target point of the destination (`T0 + 3/8 × (T1 − T0)`) without taking the source under that middle, then the rest shared so that both end at the same relative position between those points and their T1; nothing under the smallest lot (5 barrels, 3 bushels). The score is the gap between the factor the destination sells at, at the projected stock, and the one the source buys at, times the good's weight; a score of 0 or below is no transport, and the length of a trip divides nothing. The final destination is the reachable city with the highest total over the goods of the score from home, or, for a good that home does not send there, of the score back home (what the ship would bring back). A stopover is a city whose detour is under 1.2 times the direct trip home (`DetourLimit`), valued by the score from the port the ship is in; on a tie, the shortest detour. `Load` takes the goods by decreasing score, each as many units as the quantity, the stock and the room allow. What a ship loads for a port is recorded, per ship, as under way to it until the ship enters that port, cut down to what is left aboard after a sale and cleared when the ship is removed; every ship's records count in every decision (`AiMerchantFleet.UnderWayTo`), the deciding ship's own included, so that it does not buy again what it already carries for home, and two ships do not supply the same lack.
- At the start of a game every city holds the same days of every good, so no trade pays: the AI ships wait in port for the first days.

### Debug tools

- Debug tools are in the game, not in the Editor's windows, and exist only in the Editor and in development builds (`Debug.isDebugBuild`). They read the simulation and change nothing, with one exception: the buttons of the ships tab, which add and remove the ships of the AI merchants. Their UI is in `Assets/UI/Debug`, in French like the rest, in a `UIDocument` of its own on the same `PanelSettings`, so the map takes no click or wheel step on it, with a sorting order of 100, so it is drawn over the panels of the game. There is one panel (`DebugPanelController`, `DebugPanel.uxml`) with a tab per tool, "Villes" and "Navires"; it opens on the tab it was last on.
- F1 opens and closes them: `WorldMapInput` reads the key (`DebugKey`), raises `DebugToggled` and leaves it out of `AnyInput`, so that a debug tool can be opened and watched during fast forward. A new debug key goes the same way.
- `MarketWeekLog` (Core) is what a city actually produced and consumed of each good on each of the last days, a week of them at most. `CityMarket` owns one and writes it in `StartDay`: a stock above T3 cuts the production, an empty stock cuts the consumption, and what the player buys or delivers is not counted. `RecentProductionOf` / `RecentConsumptionOf` are its sums (partial during the first week); `NominalWeeklyProductionOf` is what the workshop makes with the workers of the moment and `NominalWeeklyConsumptionOf` what the population of the moment consumes, over the days of the week, which a market is given when it is created.
- The cities tab has one row per city of the map (`DebugCitiesTable`) with its population, how much it has grown or declined since the game started ("Croiss. totale", `CityPopulation.GrowthSinceStart`) and, per good of the catalogue, the stock in whole units and the weekly production, consumption and net gain, written "nominal (recent)" with one decimal, the nominal production at the workers of the moment, the net in green or red (rich text), and the workers of its workshop over their maximum ("Ouv.", "45/60", a dash for a good the city does not produce). A last row gives the total of every column: the net of the world, among others, and the growth of all the inhabitants together. The table is built the first time the panel is opened, since the economy starts after the panel is enabled, and listens to the markets' `Changed` only while its tab is shown.
- The ships tab has one row per ship of `ShipsView` (`DebugShipsTable`), the player's first: its name, its owner ("Joueur" or "IA"), what it is doing ("Au port", "En mer", "À l'arrêt"), where it is and where it goes (a city's name, otherwise a normalized position), for an AI ship its home city ("Attache") and its final destination ("Destination finale", "—" while it has none; the "Destination" column is still the next port), its crew, the share of its speed it sails at, its hold (barrels of room), the units aboard of every good and, for an AI ship, its cumulated profit (`AiMerchants.TryGetProfit`). It is rewritten every frame while it is shown, since ships sail every frame, and not otherwise.
- The ships tab has two buttons. "Ajouter un navire IA" (`AiMerchants.AddShip`) spawns one more AI ship homed in the city among those that reach another with the fewest AI ships homed there, at random among ties (`HomePortChoice`), in its port; its number is the next one, never one that a removed ship had. "Retirer", at the end of the row of every ship that is not the player's (`AiMerchants.RemoveShip`), takes the ship off the map with its cargo, which is lost: the fleet forgets it (`AiMerchantFleet.Remove`), then `ShipsView.RemoveShip` deselects it, takes it out of its port (`ShipDocking.Remove`, which raises `Undocked`, so the panels that list it follow), stops reporting its arrivals (`ShipArrivals.Forget`) and destroys its view. The button of the player's ship is hidden, and `AiMerchants.RemoveShip` refuses a ship that is not its own: nothing could make another player ship. "Ajouter un navire IA" is disabled while `AiMerchants.CanAddShip` is false: before the merchants have started, and on a map with no city to start in.

## Conventions

- **Language**: all code is in English — identifiers, comments, log messages, asset and folder names. Conversation with the user is in French.
- **Interface language**: everything the player reads is in French — UXML texts, the strings the controllers write, and the names and descriptions held by the data assets (and the defaults `WorldMapSetup` gives them). Numbers are written the French way (thousands separated by a no-break space). There is no localization system: the French text is written directly where the English one would be. City names are proper names and are not translated; the invented month names are French-sounding ("Janvis", "Févrin", …).
- **UI**: UI Toolkit (UXML + USS) for all runtime UI. Do not build uGUI Canvas hierarchies, even though `com.unity.ugui` is installed.
- **Data**: game content (goods, cities, ships, prices, ranks, etc.) is defined in ScriptableObject assets, not hard-coded. ScriptableObjects hold static definitions only; mutable simulation state lives in separate runtime objects, never written back into the assets.
- **Testing**: keep simulation logic (economy, trade, progression) in plain C# classes with no `MonoBehaviour` or scene dependency, and cover it with EditMode tests. MonoBehaviours are thin adapters that drive the simulation and present its state.

## Commands

There is no CLI build or lint step; compilation happens in the Unity Editor. The `.sln` / `.csproj` files at the root are generated by Unity and git-ignored — never edit them by hand.

Editor path: `C:\Program Files\Unity\Hub\Editor\6000.6.4f1\Editor\Unity.exe`

Tests use the Unity Test Framework (`com.unity.test-framework`); EditMode tests are in `Assets/Tests/EditMode`. Batch-mode runs require the project to be closed in the Editor. `Unity.exe` is a GUI executable, so `& $unity ...` returns before the run ends; wrap it in `Start-Process -Wait -PassThru` and read `ExitCode` (`0` passed, `2` test failures, `1` compile error) when the result matters:

```powershell
$unity = "C:\Program Files\Unity\Hub\Editor\6000.6.4f1\Editor\Unity.exe"

# All EditMode tests (use -testPlatform PlayMode for PlayMode tests)
& $unity -batchmode -projectPath . -runTests -testPlatform EditMode -testResults Logs\TestResults.xml -logFile Logs\test.log

# Single test, fixture, or namespace
& $unity -batchmode -projectPath . -runTests -testPlatform EditMode -testFilter "Namespace.ClassName.MethodName" -testResults Logs\TestResults.xml -logFile Logs\test.log
```

When the Editor is open, prefer the `unity-mcp` MCP tools (`Unity_RunCommand`, `Unity_GetConsoleLogs`, scene/camera captures) to compile, check console errors, and inspect the scene.

## Project configuration

- **Rendering**: URP 17.6 with the **2D Renderer**, linear color space. A single quality level, `PC`, uses `Assets/Settings/PC_RPAsset` → `Renderer2D`. Sprites are lit by 2D lights (`Light2D`); 3D lights and 3D-only URP features (SSAO, GPU Resident Drawer, shadow cascades) do not apply.
- **2D**: the Editor's default behavior mode is 2D (textures import as sprites). The `com.unity.feature.2d` feature set is installed (tilemaps, sprite tooling, Aseprite/PSD importers, 2D animation). No Pixel Perfect Camera is set up yet.
- **Input**: the new Input System is the only active handler — the legacy `UnityEngine.Input` API will throw. `Assets/InputSystem_Actions.inputactions` is registered as the project-wide actions asset. The game uses its `WorldMap` action map (Point, Click, PanDrag, PanMove, Zoom, Cancel, Command — the right mouse button, which gives orders and never pans); the `Player` map is the template's action-game default and is unused. `WorldMapInput` also reads the keyboard's keys directly, to tell that some key was pressed and to read the key of the debug tools (see Debug tools).

- **Build scenes**: only `Assets/Scenes/WorldMap.unity`. Its sprites are lit by a global `Light2D`.


## Unity conventions

- Every asset has a sibling `.meta` file holding its GUID. Create, move, rename, and delete assets together with their `.meta`; otherwise references in scenes and prefabs break.
- `Library/`, `Temp/`, `Logs/`, and `UserSettings/` are generated and git-ignored. Exclude `Library/` from searches — it contains the package cache (tens of thousands of files).
- Packages are declared in `Packages/manifest.json`; package sources live read-only under `Library/PackageCache/`.
