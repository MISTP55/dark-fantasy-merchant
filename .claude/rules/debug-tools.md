# Debug tools

- In the game, not in Editor windows, and only in the Editor and development builds (`Debug.isDebugBuild`). They read the simulation and change nothing, except the ships tab's buttons that add and remove AI ships, and the header's slider, which sets `GameClock.NormalSpeedMultiplier` from x1 to x600 (not a fast forward: the map stays playable), and the header's "Construction gratuite" box (`PlayerBuilder.SetFree`, off at the start of every session), with which every building of the player is quoted at 0 gold and takes neither gold nor materials, even from a treasury in debt; a workshop still needs a city that produces its good, a building its place, and nothing it would have bought is reported to the recorder; the header's history drop-down only changes how much the record keeps. Their `UIDocument` has a sorting order of 100, over the game's panels.
- F1 (`DebugKey`) toggles them, on the map and in the city view: the panel reads it itself, since the city view switches `WorldMapInput` off. `WorldMapInput` leaves it out of `AnyInput`, so that they can be watched during fast forward. A new debug key goes the same way.
- `MarketWeekLog` (Core) is what a city actually produced and consumed over the last week, its inhabitants' consumption and its workshops' inputs apart; what the player buys or delivers is not counted.
- Removing an AI ship goes through `AiMerchants.RemoveShip`, which refuses a ship that is not its own: the fleet forgets it, then `ShipsView.RemoveShip` takes it out of its port (raising `Undocked`, which the panels follow) and off the map. A new ship's number is never one a removed ship had.
- The panel has four tabs (`DebugTabs`, each an `IDebugView`): "Villes" (population, markets of one good or all), "Courbes" (by city, by indicator, composed), "Échanges" (flows of one good between the cities), "Navires". There is no journal of events or trades: the user found them of no use, so nothing records them. The views of the past are made once the record exists. The shown view is refreshed at most once a frame, when the record has new days; the ships tab every frame, as before.
- `SimulationRecorder` (debug builds only) records from the start, panel open or closed: a `SimulationRecord` (Core) of every city with a market, fed by `WorldEconomy.DayEnded` (raised after every city's `StartDay`), `AiMerchants.Traded`, `CityInfoPanelController.PlayerTraded` (market trades) and `PlayerBuilder.MaterialBought` (construction purchases). A trade belongs to the last day recorded. Its horizon (`HorizonChoices`, 12 months by default) lasts for the session; shortening it trims the history and the trades together, never the last day.
- `CityMarket.LastDay` (`CityDayReport`) is what the city needed and consumed in its last `StartDay`. Filling it must not change a figure of the simulation: it only copies values the day already computes.
- A shortage ("Manque") is what the inhabitants needed and did not get (`MissingOf`), not a stock at 0 in the evening: the city consumes before it produces.
- What the workshops take as inputs is apart from the inhabitants' consumption ("Conso."), in "Intr." (`NominalWeeklyInputNeedOf`, `RecentInputsOf`); "Net" takes both off. A production brought down by a short input shows as a real production under the nominal one.
- `Construction` reports the materials it buys through an optional callback; they count as shipped from the city.
- Trades are kept in a `TrimmedList` (constant-time trimming): with `List.RemoveRange`, a day's cost grew with the number kept.
- `TimeSeriesChart` writes its scale at its left (`ChartMath.NiceScale`, round steps) and, at its right in its colour, that of the first curve with a scale of its own that the left does not write; without a curve on the chart's scale, the left writes the first curve's own. Its dates (`ChartTimeAxis`) are in days up to two months, in months up to three years, in years beyond. Its legend leaves out a single colour; the goods of "Par ville" share one legend, and "Composer" has its chips instead.
- What produces a good is framed (`CityDefinition.ProductionOf`, through `SimulationRecorder.Goods`), bright when efficiently, darker when inefficiently: in "Par ville", the charts of the city's goods, on the choice of a city; in "Par indicateur", with a good's figure, the charts of the cities that produce the good, with a line that explains it. The enlarged chart does not carry the frame.
- A click on any chart of "Courbes" enlarges it over the whole tab (`ChartZoom`, attached by the panel to every `TimeSeriesChart` of the tab once its views exist): the large chart is the small one's `Mirror`, given its data at every `SetData`, with its legend. A click anywhere on it, a change of sub-tab or leaving the tab closes it.
- A dashed line is measured along its length from anchors on days of the game (`ChartMath.DashPath`, every power of two days of at least 24 pixels), dashed backwards before the first one. Measured from its first point, its dashes crawled along the curve each time a day came or went, which flickered; cut by days instead of length, they were distorted on a compressed, jagged curve.
- Editing the panel's USS or UXML in Play mode rebuilds its tree, and editing a script reloads the whole game: restart Play mode before checking it.
