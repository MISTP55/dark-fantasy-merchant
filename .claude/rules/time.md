# Time and fast forward

- `GameClock` (Core) cuts the simulated time of a frame (real time multiplied by the fast forward's multiplier, or else by `NormalSpeedMultiplier`, 1 but in the debug tools) into equal steps of at most `MaxStepDays`, and raises `Stepped` for each, after the `DayStarted` of the days the step starts. Everything simulated advances with `Stepped`, never once per frame with `DeltaTime`: what is checked once per update (an arrival, the end of a stopover) would otherwise lose up to a frame of time, and at high speed several days of economy would pass with no ship moving between them. A speed change by a listener applies from the next step. `MaxStepsPerAdvance` drops the time it cannot hold rather than freeze the game.
- What a simulated day costs is mostly the AI ships' departures. Since they follow the routes kept by `PortTravelTable` rather than asking the pathfinder, it was about 0.5 ms in the Editor with 48 AI ships (26 ms before), x600 (20 days a second) costing about 10 ms a second; it grows with the number of AI ships. Past what the processor can keep up with, the frame rate falls (Unity's `maximumDeltaTime` bounds each frame).
- `Time.timeScale` stays at 1, so the camera and the UI stay in real time; do not speed the game up through it.
- `WorldClock` is optional for `ShipsView`, `WorldMapInteraction` and `WorldMapCameraController`; `PlayerExpenses`, `WorldEconomy` and `AiMerchants` need it.
- Fast forward is a mode in which the map is only watched; the debug tools' faster normal speed is not one, and the map stays playable at it. Fast forward keeps its own multiplier, so it is slower than a normal speed sped up past it. `WorldMapInteraction` clears hover and selection when it starts and ends it on `WorldMapInput.AnyInput`, then calls `ConsumeInput`, so that the same input gives no click, order or zoom, not even through the release of the button (`PointerGesture.PressWithoutClick`). The HUD's button is over the UI: it does not raise `AnyInput`. Fast forward also ends when the ship selected and under way (`ShipDocking.IsUnderWay`: at sea and moving, or arriving or leaving through a harbour) when it started arrives (`awaitedShip`): docked at its berth, the city view of that port opens; in a city's queue, the city is selected and its panel opens; stopped at sea, the camera shows a close-up of it. Entering the grid does not end it, nor does a ship lying still at the anchorage while it waits or crosses the harbour: arrival is read from `StateOf`, not from `IsMoving`. However it ends, the previous selection is restored, or the city the ship is in (`IsInCity`).
- Fast forward and the city view. `GameClock.FastForwardChanged` is a plain C# event: it fires for `WorldMapInteraction` even while that component is switched off by the open view. Starting fast forward with the view open: `CityViewLoader` (which listens to it) closes the view without selecting its city again, and the interaction clears the (already empty) selections; the map is switched back on when the unload ends, with nothing selected, as fast forward wants. The view cleared the ship selection, so the interaction takes from the loader (`CityViewLoader.TakeShipUnderWay`) the player's ship that was selected and under way when the view opened: still under way, it is the ship to select again and the awaited ship, exactly as if it had been selected on the map. A ship selected in the view (through the map's ship selection, see `city-view.md`) is the ship to select again instead, but it is awaited only when it is a ship of the player under way; otherwise the loader's ship is still the one awaited (`WorldMapInteraction.AwaitedShipOf`). Both handlers of `FastForwardChanged` may run in either order: the loader's close keeps the remembered ship, and the end of the unload forgets it. When the awaited ship arrives docked, `Update` ends fast forward first (the selection is restored: the port's city is selected), then `CityViewLoader.Open(port)`, which clears those selections again and switches the interaction off, and `Update` returns at once; the view ends open on the port with nothing selected on the map. An arrival outside a port, or a missing loader, keeps the close-up. Closing that view selects the port's city, as for any view.
