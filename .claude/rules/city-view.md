# City view

## The grid

- `CityGrid` (Core) is a rectangle of cells; a cell is a `Vector2Int` with x to the right and y up, (0, 0) at the smallest corner. Its forbidden cells (`IsForbidden`, the layout's `ForbiddenCells`, painted by the layout window's Water tool) are its water: only the port may cover them; `CanPlace` refuses them for every other building.
- A `Footprint` is given once, in its `Along` orientation (`Width` on x, `Depth` on y); `Across` swaps the two. `GridPlace.Origin` is the covered cell of smallest x and y, whatever the orientation. A square footprint has one orientation: `GetPlaces` lists it `Along` only.
- `CanPlace` refuses a footprint with a side under 1 (`default(Footprint)` is 0 by 0) and `Place` throws for it: it would give room with no place.
- The grid only knows footprints, kinds and owners; it never knows who builds, what it costs or why. Rules about that (money, materials, class housing) stay in the callers.
- `BuildingKind.HousedClass` throws for a workshop and `Good` for a house: test `IsHouse` first.
- `GetPlaces` lists by x then y, `Along` before `Across`; callers that pick "the first place" rely on that order.

## The walls

- A wall is a 1×1 city building (`BuildingKind.Wall`, the catalogue's `WallDefinition` "Mur", validated 1×1), placed only by the layout: hand-painted with the layout window's Wall tool or generated; nobody builds it. It is not a road, not part of the network, never connected nor darkened, and has no room or jobs.
- The grid's enclosure (`HasClosedWalls`, `IsEnclosed`) is the flood from every cell of the town hall by sides only, never onto a wall nor onto water; the other buildings are crossed. Two walls meeting by a corner close the way. Reaching any edge cell makes the walls open, so a town hall touching the edge never has closed walls, and a grid without a town hall has none. It is recomputed lazily when a wall or the town hall is placed.
- `KeepsInsideWalls` is off at creation and on for a grid in play: `TryCreateGrid` and `StartingLayout` turn it on once the walls are checked. While on, `IsBuildable` (inside, land, enclosed) refuses every cell outside the walls; it is the one test of `CanPlace` (so `GetPlaces` and every strategy), `RoadPlanner` and `CityMarket.TryCollectNewRoads` (the player's roads). A new placement test must go through it. A wall itself is never refused for the walls. The layout window's grids (`CityLayoutEditing.TryMakeGrid`) keep it off, so that a building may stand outside the walls while a layout is edited.
- `TryValidateWalls` gives the problem that makes a grid unusable: walls that do not close, or a building whose land cell is outside them (the port's water rows are not checked).
- The layout window's Wall tool (after Water) is a brush: the left click paints walls (`CityLayoutEditing.SetWalls`), the right click erases them, and Erase erases them too. A cell is one thing at a time: walls skip water (berths included), the town hall, the port, houses and roads, and water, roads, houses, the town hall and the port skip walls. Every edit carries the walls (`Apply`, kept inside the new size). The window warns, from `TryValidateWalls` on its grid, when the walls do not hold the city.

## The port

- Every city has one port, a 4×3 building (`PortDefinition`, the catalogue's `Port`) placed only by its layout, in the layout window's Port tool; nobody builds it. A layout without one, or whose port cannot stand, is unusable (`TryCreateGrid` fails); `CityViewContentTests` checks that every city's layout is usable.
- It is placed through `CityGrid.PlacePort`/`CanPlacePort` with a `PortFootprint` (the footprint and its `WaterRows`, an asset value) and a `PortSite` (origin and `PortFacing`), never through `Place`, which throws for it. Its front `WaterRows` rows, on the side it faces, must all be water and its back rows all land (`PortFootprint.IsWater`). North is +y, east +x; facing north or south it lies `Along`, east or west `Across` (`PortFacings.OrientationOf`); R turns it a quarter turn north → east → south → west (`PortFacings.Turned`). `PlacedBuilding.PortFacing` keeps the facing.
- It is not part of the road network: not a root, a road touching it is not a network road for that, and it is never connected nor darkened. No connection to the town hall is needed.
- The layout window's edits never move it: the water brush leaves the cells under it, roads and the town hall cannot go on it, and every edit carries it (`CityLayoutEditing.Apply`).

- The grid also holds the port's berths (`Berths`, in placing order; `PlaceBerth` throws where `CanPlaceBerth` refuses): water cells where ships dock, never under the port, and `CanPlacePort` refuses a site covering one. `IsNavigable` is water with no building on it (the port is the only building on water); berths are navigable.
- `HarbourPaths` (Core) sails over the navigable cells: `TryFindExit` is an A* with 8 moves, no corner cutting (a diagonal needs both orthogonal cells navigable), whose exit is the nearest navigable edge cell, with a fixed neighbour order and ties by insertion so a grid always gives the same path. `TryValidate` gives the problem that makes a grid unusable for ships (no berth, port not joined to the edge by water, a berth with no way to the edge); a grid without a port counts as joined. Its `Moves`, `CanStep`, `StepLength` and `GetPortSides` are the only definition of how ships sail the water: `StartingBerths` measures its distances and `Harbour` its steps with them, so that the berths chosen are the nearest by sea and a ship's speed matches its path's length. Never copy them.
- The layout window's Berth tool (after Port) adds a berth with the left click on a free water cell (`CityLayoutEditing.AddBerth`, ghost green when `CanPlaceBerth`) and removes it with the right click (`RemoveBerth`); it shows "Berths: N" and, from `HarbourPaths.TryValidate` on the grid `TryMakeGrid` makes, a warning when ships cannot use the layout. `CityLayoutEditing.Apply` keeps only the berths inside the new size, on water and not under the new port, so resizing, freeing water and placing the port drop the berths they invalidate in the same undo step. `TryMakeGrid` places the berths and fails when one cannot stand.
- A layout is usable for the game (`WorldMapView.IsUsable`, see `world-map.md`) when `TryCreateGrid` succeeds: a town hall, walls that stand, a port that stands, berths that stand, roads and houses that stand, closed walls with every building inside them (`TryValidateWalls`), and `HarbourPaths.TryValidate` finds no problem.
- `Tools > Dark Fantasy Merchant > Regenerate All City Layouts` (`CityLayoutRegeneration.RegenerateAll`, with a confirmation; `Run` is the same without it) regenerates the layout of every city of the map (`CityLayoutEditing.Regenerate`, seed = index in `MapCities`, the map's mask). Run it whenever what makes a layout usable changes.

## Roads and the town hall

- The road network is kept by the grid, not recomputed by callers: a road is a 1 by 1 building, the town hall is the grid's `TownHall`, and a road is a network road (`IsNetworkRoad`) when joined to the town hall from touching cell to touching cell. The town hall alone connects nothing: a house or workshop is connected when it touches a network road.
- What touches is the grid's `Contact` (`RoadContact`), given at its creation from `BuildingsDefinition.RoadContact` and never changed: by a side or a corner, or by a side only. Every neighbour test of the network, of `RoadPlanner` and of `RoadsidePlacement` goes through `CityGrid.Touches`; a new one must too, or the two contacts disagree. The layouts are connected by sides, so that they work under either contact (`CityViewContentTests` checks both).
- `PlaceRoads` floods the network once for a whole trace; placing roads one by one floods once each. Use it for any group of roads.
- `BuildingConnected` is the only way room and hiring start. `BuildingPlaced` is raised before the flood: a `BuildingPlaced` handler must not read `IsNetworkRoad` or connection state; refresh on `BuildingConnected` (once per house or workshop, after the `BuildingPlaced` that caused it).
- `RoadPlanner` finds the fewest free cells that make a building touch the network (`TryFindRoads`), and `Connects` checks an already chosen set of roads: anything that places a building together with its roads checks `Connects` before taking anything. `RoadsidePlacement` is the strategy the cities build with (the places needing the fewest roads, at random among them); packing houses next to the roads, it walls the network in after a dozen houses or so. `RandomPlacement` ignores roads; only `StartingHouses` uses it, as its first step.
- A layout without a town hall is unusable (`TryCreateGrid` fails, the city gets no grid).

## Generated layouts

- The layout window's "Regenerate layout" (`CityLayoutEditing.Regenerate`, one undo step, nothing changed when it fails) replaces a layout's whole content, keeping its size, by `CityLayoutEditing.TryGenerate`, which the scene setup's default layout uses too: water, port, berths, town hall, walls, houses and roads, in that order (`StartingLayout.TryBuild`). The seed is the city's index in the map.
- The water is a band of `GeneratedWaterRows` rows along the edge on the side of the sea: `ShoreSide.Of` sums the offsets of the navigation mask's water within 10 mask cells of the city, the larger axis winning (south without a mask or water). The port goes `PortPlacement.TryNear` the middle of the shore; the town hall in the middle of the shore too, lying as the port does (its width along the shore), `GeneratedTownHallDistance` rows of land between it and the water (`TownHallPlacement.TryNear` that place only when it is taken or off the grid).
- The walls are on the outline of the square of `GeneratedWallSide` (45) cells centred on the town hall (a cell more on the far side, as the houses' square), on every cell of it inside the grid, land and free; the water closes them on the shore side. Generation fails, with `TryValidateWalls`'s problem, when they do not close (a grid too small for the square) or leave the port outside (a square closing on land short of it), and the grid then keeps inside them, so the houses and their roads stand inside. The 80 by 80 grids leave the square's three land sides inside the grid.
- The berths are the `GeneratedBerths` (10) navigable cells nearest the port by sailing distance (`StartingBerths.Place`: Dijkstra from the water touching the port, ties by y then x, a cell only if it has an exit); fewer when the water runs out, and generation fails ("its port is not joined to the edge") when none fits.
- The houses are just those `StartingRoomOf` asks for, placed by `StartingHouses.TryPlace`: all at random first, inside the square of `GeneratedHouseArea` cells centred on the town hall (`RandomPlacement` with an area), then roads, the house needing the fewest first, then every house no road reaches taken off (`CityGrid.RemoveHouse`, internal and silent: only for a grid nobody watches yet) and placed again by `RoadsidePlacement`, which may put them outside the square. Placing them along the roads from the start walls the town hall in long before a city's houses are placed.
- Generation always uses `RoadContact.SidesOnly`, whatever the catalogue's contact, so that the layout works under either.
- A change to what makes a layout usable (`TryCreateGrid` and what it checks) comes with the regeneration of every city's layout (`CityLayoutEditing.TryGenerate`), in the same work: the existing layouts are never patched to meet the new rule, nor left unusable for the designer to fix by hand.

## Projection

- `IsoProjection` (Core): cell (0, 0) is at the bottom of the screen, x goes to the upper right and y to the upper left, so that the grid as the layout window shows it (x east, y north) appears turned, not mirrored: its north at the upper left, its east at the upper right. The nearer of two blocks is the one beyond the other towards smaller x or y. `CellAt` is the exact inverse of `CenterOf` on every point of a cell's diamond. `PointOf` is `CenterOf` continued between cells (a grid point: cell (x, y)'s centre is (x, y)) and `GridPointAt` its exact inverse; ships use them. `DrawKeyOf` orders buildings for drawing: a larger key is drawn later. It is a `long` and not a sortingOrder: the city view ranks the placed buildings by it and gives each sprite a sortingOrder from its rank (see `CityGridView` below); ground cells sit below all buildings. The key is lexicographic, the centre (`2 * (origin.x + origin.y) + size.x + size.y`, smaller drawn later, not the far cell, which ties for neighbours of different sizes) then an unclamped tie-break on the far corner's side of the diagonal (same-shape neighbours tie on the centre); it must not be clamped or squeezed into a short (a staircase of same-shape blocks needs ~400 tie-break values). Good up to 1000 by 1000. `DrawKeyOf_OrdersEveryFrontBlockAfterTheOneBehindIt_*` brute-force 1×1, 2×1, 1×2, 2×2, 3×2, 2×3, 4×3 and 3×4 blocks (origins 0..19 and the far corners of a 200×200 grid); a new footprint size needs them extended.
- `PlacementCursor` (Core): the footprint being placed; `Start` always begins `Along`, `Rotate` does nothing when not placing or for a square footprint.

## The market on its grid

- A class's room is its connected houses on the grid × the room of a house of the class; never `AddRoom` outside a placed house when the market has a grid. `CityMarket` listens to `CityGrid.BuildingConnected`: a house gives its room when it connects (at once when it touches a network road), and keeps it waiting until then (the player's: the room it was built with; the city's and the starting ones, connected later: its class's housing rule, which a grid holding an unconnected house must have). Only the houses the market places, or that are on the grid at its creation, give room: a house placed on the grid directly afterwards gives none. A player's workshop on the grid hires only while connected. The city's houses (`CityMarket.BuildCityHouse`, materials checked, then a place and its roads found and checked with `RoadPlanner.Connects`, then taken: the house comes with its roads and is not built when no place can be connected) and the player's (`Construction.TryBuildHouse`/`TryBuildWorkshop` with a place, checked before anything is paid) are the only ways to build.
- A market with a grid needs a placement strategy and a footprint on every housing rule (`CityHousingRule.Footprint` is nullable, required only then); without a grid the placeless `AddPlayerHouse`/`AddPlayerWorkshop` apply, and with one they throw.

## Grid data (Game)

- A building asset has a `footprint` (Along: x = width, y = depth), each side at least 1 (`TryValidate`); the scene setup fills an unset (0, 0) one: 2 by 1 for houses, 2 by 2 for workshops. Never build a `Footprint` from it without validating (`default(Footprint)` is 0 by 0 and skips validation).
- `BuildingsDefinition.PlacementStrategy` (a `PlacementStrategyDefinition`, `Assets/Data/Buildings/Placement/`) is required: `WorldEconomy` makes one strategy for the whole world from it. The scene setup gives the roadside one to a catalogue with none or with the random one.
- `CityLayoutDefinition` (`CityDefinition.Layout`) is a city's grid: size, water cells, town hall, walls, port, roads, starting houses with their place and berths; `TryCreateGrid` places the town hall, the walls, the port, the berths, the roads, then the houses, then fails with `TryValidateWalls`'s problem and last with `HarbourPaths.TryValidate`'s, so a layout without a berth, or whose port or berth ships cannot reach, is unusable. The long `SetContent` takes the berths, then the walls (optional: none when null); `SetContent(grid)` keeps the grid's. `TryCreateGrid` gives the grid; a city without a usable layout is inactive (`world-map.md`, `WorldMapView.IsUsable`). `WorldEconomy` still gives such a city a market without grid, with one warning, only when the map view has no `buildings` to refuse it (it has then logged an error). `WorldEconomy.GridOf` returns the grid.
- With a grid, the room of a class at the start is its connected houses on the grid × the room of a house (`EconomyDefinition.CreateMarket`); `StartingRoomOf` is the room a grid should give at least.
- The scene setup gives a city without a layout (`Assets/Data/CityLayouts/<city>Layout.asset`) a generated 80 by 80 grid (`CityLayoutDefinition.DefaultSize`, see Generated layouts); a city with a layout is never touched. A layout asset already at that path (a link lost) is linked to the city, never rewritten; the layout window's "Create layout" does the same.

## The city view scene

- `CityView.unity` is loaded additively over the map, while the simulation keeps running in the map's scene. `CityViewLoader` (map scene, `World`) is the only way in and out: the city panel's "Voir la ville" calls its `Open`, the view's "Retour à la carte" its `Close`. Never load or unload the scene another way, or the map stays switched off or reacts under the view.
- Opening clears the map's hover and selections (its panels close) and switches off the map camera's object, `WorldMapInput` and `WorldMapInteraction`: the map would otherwise pick, pan and give orders under the view, since the city view reads the same mouse. Closing switches them back on and selects the city again, whose panel reopens, except when fast forward closed the view or is on when the unload ends: the map is then only watched and nothing is selected, the decision being taken when the unload ends. A request that arrives while the scene loads or unloads is kept, the latest winning, and carried out when it is done.
- Opening also remembers the player's ship that was selected on the map and under way (null otherwise), since the view clears that selection: fast forward started from the view awaits it, through `CityViewLoader.TakeShipUnderWay` (see `time.md`). A normal close forgets it, and so does the end of any unload.
- The view's objects reach the world only through `SimulationServices.Current`; the loader hands itself to `CityViewController.Show(city, loader)`.
- The map's `AudioListener` is on its camera's object, which is off while the view is open; the view's camera carries the only other one.
- The scene has no `Light2D`: every sprite of the view uses the unlit sprite material (`Assets/Art/CityView/CityViewSprite.mat`). Its sprites are on the `CityView` layer (made by the setup), the only one its camera draws: the map's objects stay at the same world positions under it.
- `CityViewInput` is the only reader of the `CityView` action map. Click and PanDrag are both the left button: a press released where it started is a click, one moved past the threshold pans. While `TracesWithLeftButton` is on (the road is placed), the left button raises `TraceStarted`/`TraceMoved`/`TraceEnded` instead, and neither clicks nor pans; `MiddlePan` (the middle button) pans either way. Setting `TracesWithLeftButton` drops the gestures in progress, a trace with `TraceCancelled`. It ignores pointer input over any document of the shared panel, the HUDs included: a press there starts nothing.
- `CityGridView` draws the ground in one mesh (`GroundMesh`, see below) at sortingOrder 0, below every building, and one sprite per building, ranked by `BuildingDrawOrder` (the order of `IsoProjection.DrawKeyOf`) and given `FirstBuildingSortingOrder + 2 × rank` as sortingOrder: the stride of 2 leaves a gap below each building for the ships (`ShipSortingOrderAt`, below); a building placed while the view is open is inserted in its rank, nothing but the ships is redrawn every frame. `FirstBuildingSortingOrder` is 3, so that the lowest ship order (2) stays above the ground (0). The placeholder sprites (`Assets/Art/CityView/`) follow the projection (the 2×1 block's long side goes to the upper right) and have their pivot at the centre of the footprint on the ground, where `IsoProjection.CenterOf(origin, size)` puts them; a footprint size other than 2×1, 1×2, 2×2, 3×2 and 2×3 has no block. The port has its own two placeholders, `portBackSprite` (water rows at the far side: facing north) and `portFrontSprite` (near side: facing south), drawn for a 4×3 port with 2 water rows; mirrored (`flipX`), they face east and west (`CityGridView.PortSpriteOf`). It is tinted `portColor`, ranked like any building, and never drawn with a `viewSprite`, which has only two orientations. A wall is drawn with `wallSprite` (`Wall.png`, a 1×1 block 2 cells high, 64 pixels), tinted `wallColor`, scaled by `buildingScale` and ranked like any 1×1 block. Roads are cells of the ground tinted `roadColor` (not in `BuildingDrawOrder`); a house or workshop that is not connected is darkened (`inactiveShade`) until `BuildingConnected`; the ghost has three states (`GhostState`: green connected, yellow unconnected, red impossible) and `ShowRoadGhost` draws a road's cells. A house's block takes its class's colour from `HouseColors` (Game), which the layout window also uses, so that both show the same; a workshop's takes the workshop colour of `CityGridView`, whoever owns it. A building's block is scaled down around that pivot (`buildingScale`), which leaves a gap between neighbours and never changes the drawing order; the ghost is scaled the same, so that it shows the building as it will stand.
- `GroundMesh` (Game) is the grid's ground in one renderer: a quad of the cell sprite per cell, around `IsoProjection.CenterOf`, textured through a `MaterialPropertyBlock` on the shared unlit material, tinted by its vertex colours (free, forbidden or road). A sprite per cell made the whole grid in view cost every frame the culling, sorting and batching of thousands of renderers. A cell tinted (`SetColor`, a road placed by the trace or the simulation) is uploaded once for all at `Apply`, which `CityGridView.LateUpdate` calls. Vertex colours are not converted to linear as a sprite renderer's colour is, and the project renders in linear space: go through `GroundMesh.VertexColorOf`, or the ground shows too light. Its quad is the cell sprite's whole rectangle around its pivot, and its UVs that rectangle on the texture: in an atlas, the sprite must be rectangle-packed and not rotated.
- A building whose definition has a `viewSprite` (found by `BuildingsDefinition.DefinitionOf`, which is why `CityGridView.Show` takes the catalogue and the economy) is drawn with it instead of a block: untinted (darkened only while unconnected), not scaled by `buildingScale` (its pixels per unit set its size), and mirrored (`flipX`) when placed in the orientation other than `viewSpriteOrientation`. Mirroring turns around the pivot, so the sprite's pivot must be at the centre of the footprint on the ground, horizontally included. The ghost stays a block.

## Building in the view

- `BuildBarController` (plain class over the view's document) shows the offers of `WorldEconomy.HouseOffers`/`WorkshopOffers`, the only offers that carry their footprint (one made elsewhere is 0 by 0, which `CityPlacementController` refuses), and in its "Routes" category the one `RoadOffer`, quoted for one cell (`PlayerBuilder.QuoteRoadCell`), its cost tip per cell. It quotes through `PlayerBuilder`, and refreshes on the market's, the treasury's and the hold's `Changed` and on `PlayerBuilder.FreeChanged` (the debug tools' free building, during which the cost tip says so), re-choosing the hold (`PlayerBuilder.HoldIn`) when a ship docks in or leaves the city; it is unbound when the view's controller is disabled.
- The workshops category lists only the workshops of the goods the city produces (`MaxWorkersOf` above 0): one of another good could never be built there, so it is left out rather than disabled.
- An offer's button is wrapped in an area that takes the pointer for it: a disabled button gets no pointer event, and its cost tip must still show.
- `CityPlacementController` is the only listener of `CityViewInput.Clicked`, `Rotated`, `Cancelled` and the trace events. Placing the road turns `TracesWithLeftButton` on, and `Cancel` or placing a house or a workshop turns it off; the ghost is one cell until a press, then the L of `RoadTrace.Of` from the cell pressed to the cell under the pointer; dragged off the grid or over the UI, the trace's ghost is kept on the last cell of the grid the pointer was on, not hidden, and a press off the grid or over the UI starts no trace; a release over the UI ends the trace and builds nothing (a release off the grid, not over the UI, lays the trace clamped to the last grid cell); the release lays it through `PlayerBuilder.TryBuildRoads` (one notification per trace) and the road stays placed. A road ghost is red when `QuoteRoads` is impossible, green when a cell is a network road or `JoinsNetwork`, yellow otherwise; a house's or workshop's is green when `WouldConnect`, yellow otherwise. Its `Hover`, `Click`, `TraceStart`, `TraceMove`, `TraceEnd`, `Rotate` and `Cancel` are public so that a play-mode check can drive it without Input System events; `Update` hovers the real pointer every frame while placing, so a driven hover lasts until the next frame.
- The ghost (`CityGridView.ShowGhost`) is drawn at the highest sortingOrder, above every building; it is hidden over the UI and off the grid, and red where the footprint does not fit, partly off the grid included, or wherever the offer's quote (`PlayerBuilder.QuoteHouse`/`QuoteWorkshop`) is impossible; otherwise green if the building would be connected, yellow if not (it can still be built). The bar raises `Refreshed` after each re-quote; `CityViewController` hands it to `CityPlacementController.Refresh`, which stops placing when the offer can no longer be paid for (gold or materials): the player sees its button disabled and the ghost gone, rather than clicks that silently do nothing.

## Ships in the view

- `HarbourView` (CityView scene, `World`) draws the ships on the shown city's grid, arriving, docked and leaving, never the waiting ones nor the berths (berths are seen only in the layout window). Its `Show(city)` takes the city's `WorldEconomy.HarbourOf` through `SimulationServices.Current`; `LateUpdate` re-places every ship every frame (after the simulation's `Update`), from `Harbour.TryGetPosition` through `IsoProjection.PointOf`, with pooled renderers. Ships are the only things of the view re-placed and re-ranked while it is open. The view gives ships no orders.
- A ship's sortingOrder is `CityGridView.ShipSortingOrderAt` of its rounded cell: `FirstBuildingSortingOrder + 2 × BuildingDrawOrder.RankOf(DrawKeyOf(cell, 1×1)) − 1`, the gap just below the first building whose key is not smaller, so it is drawn after every building behind it and before every building in front. Ships in the same gap are told apart by their z (farther back on the grid, farther from the camera). The ghost (`short.MaxValue`) stays above them.
- The placeholder `ShipCylinder.png` (made by `CityViewSetup`) is the circle inscribed in a cell, seen in the projection, raised into a cylinder, its pivot at the cell's centre on the ground; it is scaled down by `shipScale` and tinted `playerShipColor` (the player's, through `ShipsView.IsPlayerShip`) or `aiShipColor`, matching the map's tints.
- Selecting: `CityPlacementController` raises `ClickedWithoutPlacing` for a click while nothing is placed (while placing, clicks place and never select). `CityViewController.SelectShipAt` picks with `HarbourView.TryPickAt` (the click taken at the cylinder's middle height, then `HarbourView.TryPick`: the nearest drawn ship within half a cell of the grid point) and selects it through `ShipsView.Selection`: the ship panel shown is the map's own, on the shared `PanelSettings`, drawn over the view. A click on no ship clears that selection; so does a selected ship of the shown city going `AtSea` (`ShipDocking.CityStateChanged`). The controller listens to the docking from `Show`, since the services may not exist at its `OnEnable`.
